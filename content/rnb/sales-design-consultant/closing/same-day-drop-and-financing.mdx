## Sequence matters

A drop is a same-day discount, with a maximum of 10%. Offer it only after the full proposal has been presented and value reinforced. An early drop is a grading penalty: it shifts attention to a cheaper number before the customer understands the result and installation quality.

First present the complete solution, educate before price, explain the investment, and present financing alongside the proposal. Listen to the customer's response. Use Acknowledge → Understand → Educate → Reframe → Close to understand a concern before selecting a response. A discount is not a substitute for discovery or a clear explanation of value.

If you offer a same-day drop, keep it at or below 10%. State the actual discount and revised total clearly, and connect the decision back to the agreed scope and customer goals. Do not imply that a discount changes the installation standards or warranty. Ask for the business directly and let the customer respond.

Financing accompanies every proposal. Use current company-approved terms and information; do not invent rates, monthly payments, eligibility, or approval. Avoid claiming schedule scarcity or a guaranteed installation date unless the company has actually confirmed it.

<PhrasesToUse>

"Now that we've reviewed the full proposal and why it fits your goals, let's discuss the same-day option and the financing information."

"Does this address the concern you raised, and are you ready to move forward?"

</PhrasesToUse>

## If they still do not sign

Respect the response. Before leaving, agree on the next contact and give your personal number. Follow the personal cadence in the next lesson; do not treat a discount as permission to skip that commitment.

<WhyItMatters>

A late, clearly explained discount supports a close after value is understood. Accurate financing information helps the customer evaluate the proposal without misleading promises.

</WhyItMatters>
