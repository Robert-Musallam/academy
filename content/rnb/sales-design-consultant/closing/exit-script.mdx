## Leave with an agreement

First-call close remains the goal. If the customer does not sign, acknowledge their decision and understand what still needs to be resolved. Use the five-step objection pattern without arguing or inventing urgency. Confirm the remaining question, who needs to be involved, and what useful information you can provide.

Before leaving the house, agree on the next contact and give your personal number. The agreement should include when you will contact them, how you will contact them, and what you will discuss or send. Record the specific detail from the visit that will make that contact personal.

<PhrasesToUse>

"Before I go, what still needs to be resolved for you to feel comfortable moving forward?"

"Let's agree on our next contact so I can follow up on that specific question. What day, time, and contact method work for you?"

"Here is my personal number. I'll follow up as agreed about the detail we discussed today."

</PhrasesToUse>

## Follow through personally

Company automation uses email and text on days 0, 1, 3, and 7. Your personal cadence is day 0 evening text, day 2 call, day 5 photo/value touch, day 10 "reserve your spot" call, then every 2 weeks to day 60, then monthly. Personal messages come from your own number and reference something specific from the visit. Day 0 evening is an explicit personal touch even though company automation also runs that day.

Do not simply resend a generic quote or duplicate an automated message. Deliver the information you agreed to provide. If a promised call time differs from the default cadence, honor the agreed contact and record the next step.

<WhyItMatters>

An agreed contact, a useful reason to reconnect, and your personal number give the customer a clear way to continue the conversation.

</WhyItMatters>
